Every car has its own manufacturer-specified maintenance schedule, and that schedule is the actual authority. It is tuned to your engine, your drivetrain and sometimes your climate. What follows are the general ranges most vehicles fall into, useful for building a mental model of what's coming and when, or for sanity-checking what a shop tells you.

## The general schedule

| Item | Typical interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Oil & filter | 5,000–7,500 mi (conventional) / 7,500–15,000 mi (full synthetic) | Modern cars increasingly use an oil-life monitor instead of a fixed mileage — trust it over a rule of thumb |
| Tire rotation | 5,000–8,000 mi | Often paired with an oil change for convenience |
| Tire pressure check | Monthly | Cheapest maintenance you'll ever do; also improves fuel economy |
| Engine air filter | 15,000–30,000 mi or annually | Dustier climates trend toward the shorter end |
| Cabin air filter | 15,000–25,000 mi or annually | Easy to forget — it's not under the hood |
| Brake pads | 25,000–70,000 mi | Highly dependent on driving style and terrain; have them inspected at every rotation |
| Brake fluid | Every 2 years | Absorbs moisture over time regardless of mileage |
| Coolant | 30,000–60,000 mi or 5 years | Interval varies a lot by coolant type — check your manual |
| Transmission fluid | 30,000–60,000 mi | Some vehicles specify "lifetime" fluid — verify before assuming |
| Spark plugs | 30,000 mi (copper) to 60,000–100,000 mi (iridium/platinum) | Plug material matters more than vehicle age |
| Battery | Test annually after year 3 | Typical lifespan is 3–5 years, shorter in extreme heat |
| Wiper blades | 6–12 months | Replace sooner if streaking or chattering |
| Timing belt (if equipped) | 60,000–100,000 mi | Check your manual specifically — a failure here can damage the engine, and many newer engines use a timing chain instead, which doesn't need replacement on a schedule |

## Why "check your manual" isn't a cop-out

Two cars in the same class can have genuinely different intervals because of differences in oil formulation, turbocharging, cylinder count, or emissions tuning. A schedule built for a naturally aspirated four-cylinder isn't automatically safe for a turbocharged one. The ranges above are a starting mental model, not a substitute for the maintenance booklet that came with your specific car.
